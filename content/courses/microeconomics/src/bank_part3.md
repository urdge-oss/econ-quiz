@level 7 | hands-on-market | Hands on the Market | Price controls, taxes, subsidies, tariffs | 49-56
@brief Efficiency: a competitive market maximises total surplus (CS + PS). Deadweight loss is surplus that nobody gets.
@brief Price ceiling: consumers gain A and lose B, producers lose A and C, so the deadweight loss is B + C.
@brief Price supports and quotas help producers, but at a far higher cost to society than handing them the same money in cash.
@brief Tariff: consumers lose A + B + C + D, producers gain A, the state collects D, so the deadweight loss is B + C. A quota whose rents go abroad also loses D.
@brief Tax incidence depends on elasticities, not on who pays the tax office: buyers bear Es/(Es − Ed) of the tax.

@level 8 | one-seller-one-buyer | One Seller, One Buyer | Monopoly & monopsony | 57-64
@brief MR = P·(1 + 1/Ed). MR is below price, and it is positive only where demand is elastic.
@brief A monopolist sets MR = MC, then charges the highest price the demand curve allows at that output. It never operates on the inelastic part.
@brief Lerner index L = (P − MC)/P = −1/Ed: the more elastic the firm's demand, the smaller its markup.
@brief Sources of market power: elasticity of market demand, number of firms, how firms interact. Barriers: patents, licences, scale economies.
@brief Monopsony: marginal expenditure lies above average expenditure, so the buyer purchases less and pays less than under competition.

@level 9 | pricing-power | Pricing Power | Price discrimination, two-part tariffs, bundling | 65-72
@brief A single price leaves surplus on the table: buyers who would pay more, and buyers who would pay above MC but below the price.
@brief First degree: charge each buyer their reservation price. Output rises to the competitive level, and the firm captures all surplus.
@brief Second degree: different prices for different quantities (block pricing). Buyers sort themselves.

@brief Two-part tariff: entry fee + usage price. Bundling pays when reservation prices are negatively correlated. Tying meters usage.
